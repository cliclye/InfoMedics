      How to spot health misinformation

      Five quick habits that help, whether the claim comes from a website, a friend, or an AI chatbot.

      1. Check the source

        Who wrote it? Hospitals, health agencies, and universities are good signs. Be careful if the writer is selling a product or does not name anyone.

      2. Beware of miracle cures

        Words like “miracle,” “secret,” or “doctors hate this” are warning signs. No single food or pill fixes everything.

      3. Look for absolute words

        “Always,” “never,” “100%,” and “guaranteed” rarely fit real health. Most advice depends on the person.

      4. Check the date

        Health advice changes as new research comes in. Look for a recent date or a note that experts reviewed it.

      5. Ask a professional

        For symptoms, medicines, pregnancy, or anything urgent, ask a doctor, nurse, or pharmacist instead of relying on a search or chatbot.

      Remember: an AI answer can be polite, detailed, and still wrong. Confident writing is not proof.
