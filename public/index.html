        How Infomedics works

        A check takes a few seconds and has four steps. Here is exactly what happens, and what it cannot do.

        What happens in a check

        1. Read. Pasted text is used as written. For a link, our server downloads the page (public pages only, nothing behind a login) and pulls out the article text, author, date and links.
        2. Find claims. Sentences that make a health claim are picked out. Up to three are checked per submission.
        3. Search. We search PubMed for systematic reviews, meta-analyses and randomized trials, and MedlinePlus for plain-language topic pages.
        4. Weigh. If an AI model is connected, it compares the claim to the retrieved evidence and writes the verdict. It may only cite the sources we found. Red flags in the wording (miracle language, absolute words, fear tactics) are found by simple rules.

        The verdicts

          What it cannot do

          • It is not medical advice. It cannot know your health history. Ask a doctor, nurse or pharmacist about personal decisions.
          • The AI can be wrong. It sees abstracts, not full papers, and PubMed is not the whole of medicine. Use the links to read the sources yourself.
          • Without an AI model, there are no verdicts. Infomedics then shows the evidence and red flags and marks the claim “Not rated” instead of guessing.
          • Some pages cannot be read. Sites that block automated access, need JavaScript, or are PDFs will not work. Paste the text instead.
          • English only for now.

          Privacy

          Text and links you submit are sent to this Infomedics server. Search terms go to PubMed and MedlinePlus. If an AI model is connected, the claim and the retrieved evidence are sent to the AI provider. Your history stays in this browser’s local storage and you can clear it any time.

          How to spot health misinformation

          1. Check the source

            Who wrote it? Hospitals, health agencies and universities are good signs. Be careful if the writer is selling something or names no one.

          2. Beware of miracle cures

            “Miracle,” “secret,” and “doctors hate this” are warning signs. No single food or pill fixes everything.

          3. Look for absolute words

            “Always,” “never,” “100%” and “guaranteed” rarely fit real health. Most advice depends on the person.

          4. Check the date

            Health advice changes as research does. Look for a recent date or a note that experts reviewed it.

          5. Ask a professional

            For symptoms, medicines, pregnancy or anything urgent, ask a doctor, nurse or pharmacist instead of a search or a chatbot.

          Infomedics is a prototype and does not provide medical advice. Talk to a qualified clinician about health decisions. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.
